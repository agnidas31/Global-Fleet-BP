From Three Scoreboards to One Global View

bp's fleet card business ran in three regions, and each one reported its operations in its own units, with its own definitions and its own time windows. The Global Fleet KPI Report unified that fragmented data into one view, so leaders could monitor asset utilisation and transportation demand, and guide high-value commercial and safety decisions across global operations.

The project

One trusted view of fleet across ESA, ANZ and the US

The goal was one trusted view of fleet operations: how cards and volume are being used, how demand is trending in each region, and where commercial and safety attention should go. I owned the data behind the report end to end: bringing in three regions' data, standardising it into one model, and building the Power BI report.

The starting point: the same KPI meant three different things

Every region measured fleet its own way. Before anything could be compared, the differences had to be written down.

Some regions also counted only bp-branded cards while others included partner and third-party networks. A report that ignored all this would have looked comparable and been wrong.

Why the totals lied

Put the raw monthly volumes next to each other and the story seems obvious. It isn't.

ESA looks ten times the size of the US, but the units differ (a gallon is about 3.8 litres). The year-on-year column tells the real story: ESA is growing, ANZ is slipping, and the US is declining fastest. That's where attention should go.

The method: keep the definitions, make them visible

Rather than force one artificial definition on everyone, I mapped every region into the same structure, kept each region's own definition, and documented it. Unit and currency travel with the data as columns, so nothing is silently converted.

Making the definitions part of the report, not tribal knowledge, is what let leaders read every number with confidence.

Comparing like with like

Cross-region views use trends and year-on-year change, which stay meaningful when units don't match.

Indexed to the same starting point, three very different businesses can sit on one chart, and the region that needs attention stands out without any unit conversion.

Can we trust it?

Every refresh is reconciled against each region's own reporting before anyone relies on it.

“Which number is right?” stopped being a meeting topic: the validation page answers it before the meeting starts.

Why this mattered

One view to run global fleet operations

The point of the report was to give leaders one place to watch how fleet assets were being used and how transportation demand was moving, region by region, and to act on it. Getting there meant being honest about what each region's numbers meant and reconciling every figure to source. Once that was in place, the monthly pack no longer had to be assembled by hand, and commercial and safety decisions could be made from one trusted view, three times faster.
